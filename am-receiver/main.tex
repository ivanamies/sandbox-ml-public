\textbf{TL;DR.} Per-sender amplitude carries the message (+22\% to strip it). LayerNorm re-encodes the
loudness it deletes onto \textasciitilde{}16 pilot dims that readers demodulate (interaction 0.34). Every radio
dial was tried; the panel half-fits. A tiny LM grows with all dials installed for 0.72 nats.

\emph{(274 characters.)}

\section{Notation, units, and the cast}

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{The stream / wire / channel} --- the residual stream, one object. \textbf{Tap N} = the stream read after block N (GPT-2-small: 12 blocks; taps 3/6/9). \textbf{Writers} (senders): the attention heads and MLPs adding into it; \textbf{readers} consume it through a LayerNorm.
\item \textbf{AGC} --- automatic gain control: LayerNorm's per-token division by the stream's own scale. \textbf{The pilot} --- a small set of dimensions carrying the stream's scale \emph{through} LayerNorm: any direction with stable pre-LN amplitude c re-emerges post-LN at c/$\sigma$(t). \textbf{TPC} --- transmit power control: standardizing each writer's per-token output norm. \textbf{LO} --- local oscillator: a \emph{content-independent} fixed map; here, the fixed-linear part of inter-layer transport.
\item \textbf{Costs} are in nats of validation cross-entropy on held-out text; a model "pays X nats" means its loss is X above the matched baseline. Injections respect the linearity budget of [1].
\item \textbf{Seeds:} frozen-model measurements --- independent probe redraws; from-scratch training (§9--8) --- training seeds, matched token budgets. Three seeds throughout.
\item \textbf{Outlier / massive dims:} dimensions with far-above-median mean magnitude, the known extreme activations of [5]. \textbf{A} --- the amplification LayerNorm applies to ablation survivors, measured as the ratio of per-token normalization denominators between a clean and an ablated forward.
\end{itemize}
\section{0. Background and prior work}

This program's channel paper [1] measured the residual stream as a multiple-access channel; along
the way it found that roughly half of ablation "self-repair" [3, 4] is LayerNorm [2] acting as
gain control --- a result restated in full in §3, because this paper is written to stand alone.
The question here is the literal follow-up: if the receiver has AGC, where is the rest of the
radio? Channel-census rows reused in §2's table (fading, linearity, overlap, the estimation bound,
despreading) carry one-line methods there and full restatements in Appendix C. Amplitude-coded information at the per-writer level connects to superposition
[6]; the pilot dimensions this paper identifies are the massive activations / outlier dimensions
known to quantization and serving practice [5], here given a functional account; attention sinks
[5c] are the parked receivers of the tuning census. The engineering alternatives to trained
normalization --- removing it with careful scaling --- are the normalizer-free line [7]; this paper's
training-time experiments instead \emph{add} designed radio components to a standard recipe and measure
what the optimizer does with them. Models and corpora: GPT-2 [8a], Pythia [8b], TinyStories [8c].

Two places the analogy is loose, stated so nobody over-reads it. \textbf{No carrier wave}: nothing
oscillates; "AM" means amplitude-coded, and since symbol identity rides \emph{direction} while intensity
rides amplitude, the modulation is polar/QAM-shaped, not pure AM. \textbf{Broadcast vs multiple access}:
AM radio is one station per frequency; this is 156 simultaneous senders on one wire.

The defensible sentence, every clause carrying a number, a control, and a JSON: **the residual
stream is an amplitude-modulated multiple-access channel with receiver-side AGC, a learned pilot,
and readers that demodulate against it --- and a small model can be grown with the whole radio
installed.** The chain that got here: Hydra confound $\to$ AGC measured $\to$ the gain law $\to$
amplitude-is-signal $\to$ SGD opens the amplitude channel $\to$ the pilot exists $\to$ the pilot is used $\to$
the radio is grown.

\textbf{Plainly.} This table is the paper. Each row is one thing a radio engineer would demand of an
"it's a radio" claim, the experiment that checked it, and where the raw numbers live.

\section{3. The receiver has AGC, and half of "self-repair" is it}

\textbf{Claim.} Restated here in full so this paper stands alone (first reported in this program's
channel paper [1]; same JSONs). LayerNorm is automatic gain control: ablate a branch, the stream's
per-token scale drops, every downstream LayerNorm divides by the smaller denominator, and the
survivors are amplified. Measured by re-running ablations with every denominator frozen to its
unablated per-token value: non-dominant branch damage grows 2--8$\times$ (per-seed medians 2.0/2.1/6.1) ---
roughly half the celebrated ablation "self-repair" is receiver-side gain compensation, not
redundancy. The masking follows a gain law only loosely: measured amplification exceeds the
independence prediction (1--f)\textasciicircum{}(--1/2) by a consistent 2.6--3.4$\times$ because senders anti-cancel, and in
serial-residual models the masking ratio tracks measured amplification (Spearman 0.79) while
parallel models pin near 1.3 and decouple (--0.04).

\textbf{Method.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item Frozen-denominator re-ablation on mechanically-located induction heads: \texttt{ecc\_repr/agc.py::agc\_census}, hook \texttt{agc.py::LNFreeze}.
\item The gain law --- per-LN $\sigma$ ratios paired with an independent writer-decomposition estimate of each branch's variance share: \texttt{ecc\_repr/agc\_law.py::agc\_law}.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Controls.} \emph{Positive:} a synthetic unit test --- deleting a 50\%-variance independent component
must amplify survivors by $\sqrt{}$2, and does; the all-branches ablation verifies full-damage detection.
\emph{Negative:} a frozen \emph{clean} forward reproduces the clean score to 3$\times$10$^{-7}$; LayerNorms upstream of
the write show amplification exactly 1; and the random-init rerun shows the damage-surface \emph{fit}
is unrelated to the gain mechanism (the sum-shaped fit persists frozen, 0.996 vs 0.046). \emph{Loading:}
mean-ablation, not zero-ablation; gain and damage measured on identical batches, per-token paired.

\textbf{Result.} As claimed; the dominant branch is the exception (0.684 live vs 0.544 frozen). The
usable residue is a two-forward-pass diagnostic: run one clean and one ablated forward, read A
from the $\sigma$ ratios, and know --- with no damage evaluation --- which single-ablation numbers understate
damage, with \textasciitilde{}1.3$\times$ as the observed floor even where masking is flat. Raw data:
\texttt{artifacts/results/macl\_l4\_agc.json}, \texttt{agc\_law.json}.

\textbf{Scale \& generality.} GPT-2 (+ random-init control) for the decomposition, 3 seeds; the law on
GPT-2 + Pythia-70m/160m, 54 branch-rows. The gain law generalizes across wiring; the damage link
is architectural.

\textbf{Images.} None committed yet (honest gap).

\textbf{Plainly.} Cut a cable inside the network and the damage looks small --- partly because an
automatic volume control turns the survivors up before anyone measures. Hold that knob still and
the same cut costs two to eight times more. This is the observation the whole radio investigation
grew from: if the receiver has gain control, what else from the radio is in there?

\section{4. Amplitude is signal: the transmitter has no power knob}

\textbf{Claim.} Standardizing every writer's per-token output norm at inference --- mechanically
successful (per-LN $\sigma$ coefficient of variation collapses 1.5647 $\to$ 0.1007) --- costs \textbf{+22.3\%}
validation NLL. Heads-only regulation costs +11.0\% against MLPs-only +3.9\%: head amplitudes are
the information-dense ones. And SGD \emph{opens} this channel: trained writers' per-token norm CV is
0.38--0.66 against a random-init 0.10--0.16 --- training moves away from constant power, on every
model and corpus tested. With writer amplitudes pinned, the frozen-LN damage-masking ratio of [1]
collapses from 3.92 to 0.93: the AGC exists to ride exactly this informational variation.

\textbf{Method.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item Per-writer deviation-norm standardization with a $\beta$ knob, heads and MLPs separately and jointly: \texttt{ecc\_repr/tpc.py::TPC}, driver \texttt{tpc.py::a2}.
\item The writer-norm census, trained vs random-init, two corpora: \texttt{tpc.py::a1} with \texttt{generalize.py::GenWriterTap}.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Controls.} \emph{Positive:} the regulation demonstrably lands ($\sigma$ CV collapses 16$\times$), so the cost is
not an inert manipulation's. \emph{Negative:} random-init models for the census; $\beta$=0 is verified to be
the identity (\texttt{tests/test\_level\_plan.py}). \emph{Loading:} identical evaluation tokens across
conditions; calibration and evaluation disjoint.

\textbf{Result.} As claimed. Raw data: \texttt{artifacts/results/tpc\_retrofit.json}, \texttt{tpc\_census.json}.

\textbf{Scale \& generality.} Retrofit: GPT-2, 3 seeds. Census: GPT-2 + Pythia-70m/160m, trained vs
random-init, TinyStories and WikiText. Scope note, so this is not quoted against the companion
paper's "the wire is FM": that claim is about the token's \emph{total} norm (which carries almost
nothing); this one is about \emph{per-sender} envelopes (which carry a fifth of the task). The stream is
amplitude-modulated per sender and level-normalized in aggregate.

\textbf{Images.} None committed yet (honest gap); the numbers are in the JSONs above.

\textbf{Plainly.} We tried to give the transmitter a power regulator, like a radio station holding
constant wattage. The model got dramatically worse --- because the "wattage" wobble was never
sloppiness, it was the broadcast. An AM transmitter has no power knob separate from the message;
neither does this system, and its own training actively widens the amplitude swing.

\section{5. The pilot exists, and training builds it}

\textbf{Claim.} The pre-LN per-token scale is recoverable from the post-LN stream at R$^{2}$ 0.91--0.99 on
every trained model tested --- LayerNorm re-encodes what it divides out. Training's contribution is
\emph{concentration}: about 16 dimensions carry most of the recovery (top-16 R$^{2}$ 0.79--0.83 at shallow
taps, against 0.04--0.19 for random-16), while a random-init model holds the same information
diffusely (top-16 $\approx$ random-16). The carriers are the familiar massive/outlier dimensions [5],
functioning as a pilot tone.

\textbf{Method.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item Ridge probe on the actual reading LayerNorm's output predicting log $\sigma$ of its input, held out; localization via top-|mean| dims vs random dims: \texttt{ecc\_repr/pilot.py::b1}.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Controls.} \emph{Positive:} the mechanism is guaranteed for a constant direction (c/$\sigma$(t) algebra), so
the probe must fire --- total R$^{2}$ validates the probe. \emph{Negative:} shuffled-pairing null ($\approx$0);
random-init model --- which carries the information but \emph{unlocalized}, isolating what training adds.
\emph{Loading:} same tokens; held-out splits; per-model reading LNs at matched depth fractions.

\textbf{Result.} As claimed; on parallel-residual models the pilot delocalizes with depth (top-16 falls
to 0.09--0.23 deep) while total recovery stays $\ge$0.98. A retrofit attempt --- injecting an artificial
pilot on a spare direction at inference --- fails on every registered criterion (Appendix A): the
niche is occupied. Raw data: \texttt{artifacts/results/pilot\_exists.json}, \texttt{pilot\_retrofit.json}.

\textbf{Scale \& generality.} GPT-2 (+ random-init control), Pythia-70m/160m; 3 seeds.

\textbf{Images.} None committed yet (honest gap).

\textbf{Plainly.} The normalizer deletes the loudness of every token --- but anything held at constant
strength comes out the other side carrying exactly the deleted number, inverted. Trained networks
exploit this: they build a handful of always-loud dimensions that broadcast "how loud was this
token really." Those mysterious huge activations that make quantization painful? That's the
station's pilot tone.

\section{6. Readers demodulate against the pilot}

\textbf{Claim.} The pilot is used, not vestigial. Token-shuffling the 16 pilot dims at every LayerNorm
output costs +194\% NLL --- 18$\times$ a random-16 control (+10.6\%) and 5$\times$ a next-tier-16 control (+39.5\%);
freezing them to their means costs +139\%, so the \emph{pairing} with $\sigma$(t) is what is read. The
discriminating measurement is an interaction: with amplitude information already stripped by §4's
TPC, destroying the pilot adds only \textbf{0.341 $\pm$ 0.004} of its standalone cost. Two-thirds of the
pilot's functional value is amplitude demodulation; one-third is amplitude-independent and
unattributed (sink mechanics are the candidate).

\textbf{Method.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item Dim-set interventions (shuffle/freeze) on LN outputs with variance-share reporting; the TPC $\times$ pilot-shuffle interaction; a $\sigma$-probe collapse check: \texttt{ecc\_repr/pilot.py::demod}, \texttt{pilot.py::LNDimEdit}.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Controls.} \emph{Positive:} the mechanical check --- the $\sigma$-probe on the pilot dims collapses 0.807 $\to$
--0.003 under the shuffle, confirming the intervention severs the channel §5 identified.
\emph{Negative:} random-16 and next-tier-16 dim sets; the honesty row that the sets are not
variance-matched (pilot dims carry 21.4\% of LN-output variance vs 3.4\%/1.6\%) is stated, and is why
the interaction --- not the raw deltas --- is the discriminator: attention-sink plumbing would not care
whether amplitude information exists, predicting a ratio near 1. \emph{Loading:} same evaluation
tokens; marginals preserved under shuffle.

\textbf{Result.} As claimed, with seed-to-seed stability of 0.004 on the interaction. Raw data:
\texttt{artifacts/results/pilot\_demod.json}.

\textbf{Scale \& generality.} GPT-2 only (the parallel models' depth-delocalized pilot makes a dim-set
intervention undertreat them --- stated limitation), 3 seeds.

\textbf{Images.} None committed yet (honest gap).

\textbf{Plainly.} Is the pilot tone actually listened to? Scramble just those 16 dimensions and the
model falls apart --- but the decisive test is subtler: first delete the amplitude information the
pilot would help decode, \emph{then} scramble the pilot. It barely matters anymore. Two-thirds of the
pilot's job was decoding amplitudes; that's a receiver demodulating against its reference, caught
in the act.

\section{7. The dial panel, graded}

\textbf{Claim.} Taking the metaphor dial-by-dial, with a registered prediction and a refusal criterion
per dial: a \textbf{limiter} (clip the stream's whitened radius at its clean 99th percentile, sparing
the carrier token) is installable for \textbf{+0.01\%} --- free --- and even clipping at the median costs
only 2.4--3.9\%: the aggregate radius tail is slack. A \textbf{squelch} exists (shift every MLP threshold;
minimum exactly at the trained setting) but its asymmetry is the reverse of broadcast radio's:
admitting false firing costs +88--100\% at half a std while dropping weak signals costs +23--25\% ---
the signature of an interference-limited receiver, i.e. CDMA, not broadcast AM. The \textbf{tuning}
census finds no station dial: 49--50\% of heads are parked on the carrier token (median attention
mass 0.49--0.50), \textasciitilde{}1\% are content-tuned once the sink is excluded and content whitened, 35\% scan,
and induction heads are relation-locked --- correlator receivers. A \textbf{bandwidth} dial is refused
outright (Appendix A). The panel verdict: not broadcast AM --- an amplitude-modulated CDMA receiver
bank, which is the same modulation [1] found geometrically.

\textbf{Method.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item Limiter (per-tap Mahalanobis-radius clip, position 0 exempt; plus an attack demo): \texttt{ecc\_repr/dials.py::limiter}. Squelch (per-neuron-$\sigma$ bias shifts): \texttt{dials.py::squelch}. Tuning census (positional peakedness, sink-excluded whitened content resultants, parked mass): \texttt{dials.py::tuning}. Bandwidth (reader SVD truncation): \texttt{dials.py::bandwidth}.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Controls.} \emph{Positive:} each dial's throw demonstrably engages (firing rates move under squelch;
clipped fraction matches the quantile). \emph{Negative:} the limiter's clean-cost reference; the squelch
$\delta$=0 point; unwhitened content-tuning shown to be an anisotropy artifact (94\% $\to$ 1\% after
whitening --- [1]'s own lesson re-applied). \emph{Loading:} identical tokens per sweep; thresholds in
per-neuron $\sigma$ units; radius quantiles from disjoint calibration data.

\textbf{Result.} As claimed. The limiter's registered \emph{defensive} payoff failed --- an 8$\times$-amplitude
injection lives inside the clean radius envelope and its output KL is untouched (0.0141 $\to$ 0.0143)
--- so the limiter is free but not a tamper defense (Appendix A). Raw data:
\texttt{artifacts/results/dial\_limiter.json}, \texttt{dial\_squelch.json}, \texttt{dial\_tuning.json},
\texttt{dial\_bandwidth.json}.

\textbf{Scale \& generality.} GPT-2, 3 seeds per dial.

\textbf{Images.} None committed yet (honest gap).

\textbf{Plainly.} We went down the front panel of a real radio and asked, dial by dial: does this
network have one? Volume-limiter: yes, and it's free. Squelch: yes, but tuned like a crowded
party-line, where letting in babble is far worse than missing a whisper. Station dial: mostly
absent --- half the receivers just sit parked on the carrier, and the famous induction heads lock
onto \emph{patterns}, like code-division receivers, not frequencies. The panel that emerges isn't a
kitchen AM set; it's a cell-tower receiver bank.

\section{8. The mixer: born fixed, trained away, purchasable back}

\textbf{Claim.} A local oscillator is content-independent by definition, so the designated break-point
test asks whether one fixed linear map carries inter-layer transport. At random initialization it
essentially does: held-out R$^{2}$ 0.855--0.885 for a single fixed map on the transport increment.
Training tears half of it down: trained R$^{2}$ 0.470--0.546. \textbf{And it can be bought back}: adding a
per-block penalty holding transport near a learned content-independent map keeps R$^{2}$ at 0.83--0.94
through training, at a measured price of \textbf{1.27 nats} on the tiny testbed --- a main result of this
paper, not a footnote: content-dependent mixing is worth that much to a d=256 model, which is the
quantitative form of the superposition-load account (everything shares the wire, so transport must
route by content). The registered \emph{cheap-preservation} criterion ($\le$0.15 nats) did fire, and is
reported as such; the price itself is the finding, and §9 shows companion wires cut it nearly in
half.

\textbf{Method.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item The break-point test (fixed ridge map on increments, $\lambda$ selected on a validation split; shuffled-pairing null; random-init control): \texttt{ecc\_repr/dials.py::lo}.
\item The purchase (per-block penalty toward learned fixed maps M\_l, from-scratch training): \texttt{ecc\_repr/bonsai.py} condition \texttt{lo\_keep}.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Controls.} \emph{Positive:} the random-init model --- the LO must be visible where it must exist, and
is (0.86--0.88). \emph{Negative:} shuffled-pairing nulls (--0.5 to --0.6). \emph{Loading:} $\lambda$ chosen on a
train-internal validation split; matched token budgets for the training arm; the same fixed-map
statistic scores baseline and lo\_keep models.

\textbf{Result.} As claimed; the tiny from-scratch baseline lands at R$^{2}$ $\approx$ 0.51 --- the same \textasciitilde{}0.5 boundary
as trained GPT-2, a third independent replication of the half-dismantled LO. Raw data:
\texttt{artifacts/results/dial\_lo.json}, \texttt{bonsai\_score.json}.

\textbf{Scale \& generality.} Break-point: GPT-2, 3 seeds. Purchase: the §9 tiny-LM testbed (d=256, 25M
tokens), 3 seeds. Registered follow-up prediction, unrun: the price should fall with width as
superposition thins.

\textbf{Images.} None committed yet (honest gap).

\textbf{Plainly.} A radio's mixer shifts signals with one fixed reference oscillator --- it doesn't care
what the program is. A freshly-initialized transformer moves information between layers in almost
exactly that fixed way. Training then rewires half of that fixed plumbing into content-dependent
routing --- and if you forbid the rewiring, the model obliges, keeps the fixed plumbing, and hands
you the bill: about 1.3 nats. That bill \emph{is} the measurement: it's what content-routing is worth.

\section{9. Growing the radio: the full-dial model}

\textbf{Claim.} A tiny LM can be grown with every dial installed by construction --- pinned pilot
dimension, explicit per-block volume gains, preserved LO --- for \textbf{0.72 nats} (val 2.4946 $\to$ 3.2183).
The wires are strongly sub-additive: the sum of single-wire costs is 1.30 nats (pilot 2.5223,
volume 2.4919, lo\_keep 3.7655, each vs 2.4946), so installing them together discounts the total by
0.57 nats, \textasciitilde{}190$\times$ seed noise. Attribution per wire: the pilot is cheap and mechanically perfect
(designated-dim $\sigma$-recovery R$^{2}$ 0.95--0.99); the volume gains are free; the LO wire is the cost
center; and part of the discount is a softer LO hold in company (R$^{2}$ 0.62/0.71 vs 0.87/0.68 alone).

\textbf{Method.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item One trainer, one recipe (6 layers, d=256, 25M TinyStories tokens), conditions as flags: \texttt{ecc\_repr/bonsai.py::BonsaiGPT}, scoring battery \texttt{bonsai.py::score} ($\sigma$-probe adoption, pilot shuffle, outlier census, fixed-map R$^{2}$, dial throws).
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Controls.} \emph{Positive:} every wire's mechanism verified in-model (pilot dim carries $\sigma$ by
construction; LO R$^{2}$ moves; gains train). \emph{Negative --- the wrong-target control:} \texttt{pilot\_noise}
reserves the same dimension and writes fresh noise into it --- same capacity tax, no usable
reference; it costs \emph{more} than the real pilot (2.5342 vs 2.5223, disjoint seed ranges).
\emph{Loading:} matched token budgets, matched recipe, 3 training seeds per condition, identical
scoring battery.

\textbf{Result.} As claimed --- with the honest failures up front: the designed pilot is \textbf{unread} at
this scale (shuffling it costs +0.00\% in every condition; adoption failed; the emergent diffuse
broadcast suffices), and the trained volume knob adds no adjustability ($\pm$3 dB throws hurt as much
as on baseline, +33\% vs +34\%). Raw data: \texttt{artifacts/results/bonsai\_score.json}.

\textbf{Scale \& generality.} One testbed (d=256, 25M tokens); adoption in particular may be a scale
effect --- GPT-2's demodulated pilot emerged from \textasciitilde{}4 orders of magnitude more data.

\textbf{Images.} None committed yet (honest gap).

\textbf{Plainly.} We grew a small language model like a bonsai tree with wires already on the trunk: a
built-in pilot tone, explicit volume screws, and the fixed mixer plumbing held in place. The tree
grows, at a known price --- and cheaper with all wires than the sum of each alone. Two humbling
findings ride along: the tree politely grows \emph{around} the free pilot without ever using it, and
the volume screws, though free to install, still can't be turned. You can install legibility;
getting it \emph{used} is a different problem.

\section{10. Outliers are ballast: a causal account}

\textbf{Claim.} Massive-activation dimensions respond causally to stream stress. Baseline tiny models
grow 0--6 outlier dims at the mid layer; write per-boundary noise into one reserved dimension and
they grow 9--15; constrain transport to fixed maps and they grow 20--27; the full-dial model, 21--27.
Three different stressors, one response: when the stream's per-token scale is jittered or its
routing is constrained, the model builds more large stable dimensions to dominate the normalizer's
denominator. Together with §5 (the same dimensions carry the pilot in trained LMs), this gives the
outlier phenomenon [5] a functional account: gain-stabilization infrastructure.

\textbf{Method.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item Outlier census across §9's conditions (dims exceeding 5$\times$ the median post-LN mean magnitude): \texttt{ecc\_repr/bonsai.py::\_massive}.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Controls.} \emph{Positive/negative/loading:} inherited from §9's matched-budget design; the
prediction this inverts (that a pilot would \emph{suppress} outliers) is recorded in Appendix A.

\textbf{Result.} As claimed. Raw data: \texttt{artifacts/results/bonsai\_score.json}.

\textbf{Scale \& generality.} The tiny testbed, 3 seeds per condition; directionally consistent across
all three stressors, which is what the claim rests on.

\textbf{Images.} None committed yet (honest gap).

\textbf{Plainly.} Ships carry ballast to stay level in rough water. These networks appear to do the
same: shake the water --- noisy scale, constrained plumbing --- and they grow more of those huge
stabilizing dimensions. Calm water, less ballast. The infamous outlier activations aren't a bug;
they're the keel.

\section{11. The panel as a toolbox: what each dial is good for}

The bar this paper was held to: predictions and interventions that can be actually used. Dial by
dial, post-measurement:

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{AGC (LayerNorm).} Use: the two-forward-pass diagnostic of [1] --- read A from $\sigma$ ratios and know which single-ablation results understate damage (\textasciitilde{}1.3$\times$ observed floor). Consolidation path: coherent AGC via the pilot, tested in §9.
\item \textbf{Pilot (\textasciitilde{}16 outlier dims).} Use now: a \emph{named, checkable} set whose precision matters --- a selection rule and a mechanism for the mixed-precision practice of [5]; and a free runtime $\sigma$-consistency monitor (the probe of §5 is two matrix multiplies). Consolidation: reserve one dim (§9) --- installable for 0.028 nats, not yet adopted at small scale.
\item \textbf{TX power knob.} Cannot exist --- power \emph{is} the modulation (§4). The +22.3\% is the metaphor asserting itself, not breaking. Use: don't build amplitude regularizers into training; the channel is load-bearing.
\item \textbf{Volume.} Consolidated and tested: one explicit gain per layer installs for free (--0.003 nats) and still cannot be turned ($\pm$3 dB throws hurt like baseline). Use: the negative result --- in-stream level is computation; the real volume knobs are temperature and injection amplitude, outside the wire.
\item \textbf{Squelch.} Exists; sits exactly at its trained optimum; asymmetry reversed (hiss $\gg$ dropout) --- the receiver is interference-limited. Use: a one-sweep diagnostic of interference-limitation for any model.
\item \textbf{Selectivity / IF bandwidth.} Refused --- readers are rank-critical (Appendix A). Use: the negative result --- do not prune reader rank expecting interference rejection.
\item \textbf{Tuning dial.} No content dial: half the receivers are parked on the carrier, induction heads are code-locked correlators. Use: parked-mass and relation-locking as cheap head-classification features.
\item \textbf{Limiter.} Installable free at the clean q99 radius (+0.01\%), carrier exempt. Use: a zero-cost robustness clamp on the stream's dynamic range --- with the measured caveat that it is not a tamper defense (in-envelope attacks pass; that job belongs to the seal of the program's integrity paper).
\item \textbf{Mixer / LO.} Half-fixed after training, fully fixed at birth, and purchasable: hold it through training for 1.27 nats alone, 0.72 with companion wires (§§8--8). Use: legible-transport models at a known price; registered prediction that the price falls with width.
\item \textbf{AFC (drift tracking).} Honest gap: nothing tracks drift; readers re-zero per token. Flagged, no experiment.
\item \textbf{Pre-emphasis / predistortion.} Already an artifact: the companion steering paper [9] measures the distortion curve and ships the pre-warp.
\item \textbf{Antenna / impedance matching.} A flagged direction (layer-boundary statistics as impedance discontinuities), proposed elsewhere in the program, unrun.
\end{itemize}

\section{13. Limitations}

Most single-model results are GPT-2-small; the demodulation interaction is GPT-2-only; the
training-time results are one d=256 testbed at 25M tokens, and adoption in particular may simply
need scale. Costs are validation-NLL nats on TinyStories --- no downstream capability evaluation.
The sub-additivity is measured but not localized (the accretion ladder is registered, unrun). The
metaphor is scored, not assumed: where it fails --- no bandwidth dial, reversed squelch asymmetry,
no usable in-stream volume knob, a half-snapped mixer --- the failures are reported at the same
volume as the fits, and the honest composite is an amplitude-modulated CDMA receiver bank, not a
kitchen radio.

\section{Appendix A --- fired kills, refusals, and reversed predictions}

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{Cheap LO preservation ($\le$0.15 nats): killed.} The wire works (§8) at 1.27 nats alone, 0.72 in company --- reported as the price of content-dependent mixing, which is the result; the \emph{cheap} version is dead. (\texttt{bonsai\_score.json}.)
\item \textbf{TPC as sender-side regulation: killed by its own two-sided test.} The registered sloppiness-vs-signal fork landed on signal at +22.3\% (§4); regulating writer amplitude in training would fight the channel SGD deliberately opens (census CV 4--8$\times$ random-init). (\texttt{tpc\_retrofit.json}, \texttt{tpc\_census.json}.)
\item \textbf{The retrofit pilot: killed on all three registered criteria.} An artificial pilot injected at the embedding is absorbed to gain 0.05--0.07 by mid-depth, costs 1.3--1.4\% NLL, and predicts nothing about path fading (median r = 0.11). Consistent with §5: the niche is occupied. (\texttt{pilot\_retrofit.json}.)
\item \textbf{The bandwidth dial: refused.} Truncating reader input matrices to 2/3 rank already costs +47--49\%, and injected-interference sensitivity \emph{rises} under truncation --- readers are rank-critical matched filters; selectivity is not implemented as subspace width. (\texttt{dial\_bandwidth.json}.)
\item \textbf{The limiter as tamper defense: refuted.} Clipping is free (§7) but an 8$\times$-amplitude injection hides inside the clean radius envelope (output KL 0.0141 $\to$ 0.0143). The dial is a dial, not a shield. (\texttt{dial\_limiter.json}.)
\item \textbf{Squelch asymmetry: reversed.} Broadcast intuition said dropping weak signals hurts more than admitting hiss; measured, hiss costs \textasciitilde{}4$\times$ more --- the interference-limited signature that feeds the panel verdict. (\texttt{dial\_squelch.json}.)
\item \textbf{Pilot adoption (P1) and outlier suppression (P2): failed.} The designed pilot is unread at 25M tokens in every condition; and the pilot does not reduce outlier formation --- instead §10's inversion: stressors grow it. (\texttt{bonsai\_score.json}.)
\item \textbf{Volume as a turnable dial: failed in spirit.} The registered criterion (<2$\times$ baseline throw damage) is met trivially at 0.97$\times$; the knob adds no adjustability. In-stream level is computation; the real volume knobs are temperature and injection amplitude, outside the wire. (\texttt{bonsai\_score.json}.)
\end{itemize}
\section{Appendix C --- the channel census, restated for standalone reading}

One-paragraph restatements of the [1]-marked rows of §2, with their controls; full treatments and
identical JSONs live with this repository's channel paper.

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{Fading.} Inject a unit dictionary direction at quarter depth, read its surviving projection at three-quarter depth, per natural-text context, after dividing out per-position means (without that conditioning, positional structure masquerades as fading --- the registered trap). 33--41\% of contexts sit $\ge$3 dB below the median path gain and 12--23\% sit $\ge$10 dB down; fades persist $\approx$1 token. Negative control: the same heads probed on synthetic inputs show 0.8--1.2\% deep fades. (\texttt{macl\_l4pre\_fading\_natural.json}.)
\item \textbf{Linearity budget.} Two dictionary directions injected singly and jointly, response read three blocks downstream; intermodulation --- response at mixed components that only nonlinearity creates --- stays below 0.1 up to 3$\times$ typical feature amplitude (taps 3/6) and 10$\times$ (tap 9), and must and does saturate at 100$\times$ (0.41). Every injection in this paper respects the budget. (\texttt{wire\_datasheet.json}.)
\item \textbf{Deliberate overlap.} Head write-subspace overlap measured after whitening by the stream's own covariance (unwhitened, anisotropy manufactures coordination), against a random-init null: 1.3--33$\times$ above null on GPT-2; across an 8-model roster the split is the residual wiring --- serial 3.14--4.12, parallel 0.64--1.59, no overlap between groups. (\texttt{macl\_l1\_whitened.json}, \texttt{generalize\_e3.json}.)
\item \textbf{The estimation bound.} For an amplitude planted on a unit direction against the measured stream covariance, the Cramér--Rao bound is 1/(u$^{T}$$\Sigma$$^{-1}$u); the whitened matched filter empirically reaches 93--100\% of it, so the covariance genuinely describes token-level noise. Negative control: the unwhitened correlator at 0.12--0.17. (\texttt{crb\_receiver.json}.)
\item \textbf{Despreading.} A single feature rides at --28 to --30 dB per-feature SNR yet is recovered at AUROC 0.993--0.995 by correlating along its full direction --- the stream is a spread code, and the processing gain is why small readers work. Controls: planted ground truth; shuffled labels at 0.5. (\texttt{macl\_l15\_despreading.json}.)
\item \textbf{Heavy tails.} 34\% of tokens exceed the 99th-percentile $\chi$$^{2}$ reference for their Mahalanobis radius (random-init: 1--4\%). (\texttt{wire\_datasheet.json}.)
\end{itemize}
